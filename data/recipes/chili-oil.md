## Ingredients

- 1 1/2-3 cups neutral oil (350-700 ml)
- 5 star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick (preferably cassia cinnamon)
- 2 bay leaves
- 3 tablespoons Sichuan peppercorns
- 2 black cardamom pods (optional)
- 4 nuggets dried sand ginger (optional - about 1 tablespoon)
- 2 teaspoons cloves (optional)
- 3 cloves garlic (optional - crushed)
- 1-2 shallots (optional - halved)
- 3/4-1 1/4 cup Sichuan chili flakes (65-110g)
- 1 - 2 teaspoons salt (to taste)


## Instructions

1. Place the selected aromatics and oil in a pot, leaving at least 2 inches of headroom. Use 1½ cups oil with fewer aromatics or up to 3 cups with all of them.
2. Heat over medium, then lower to medium-low or low. Aim for 225-250°F (110-120°C) and slow bubbles. Lower the heat if spices darken or sizzle hard; raise it if bubbles stop. Infuse for 30-60 minutes, staying near 200-225°F when needed to prevent burning. Remove garlic and shallots once browned.
3. Place the Sichuan chili flakes in a heatproof bowl. Use ¾ cup flakes for 1½ cups oil or 1¼ cups for 3 cups oil; adjust to taste.
4. Remove very dark aromatics. Use 325-350°F (163-177°C) oil for fresh flakes, 250-275°F (120-135°C) for dry flakes, or 225-250°F (110-120°C) for roasted flakes. Test a small amount first.
5. Carefully strain the hot oil over the flakes and stir. They should smell toasted, not burnt.
6. Stir in the salt and cool. Store airtight in the refrigerator. Use a clean utensil; the oil keeps for up to 6 months.
